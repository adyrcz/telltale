Commander Kael Thorne stood at the observation window of the Meridian, watching the dying star pulse like a heartbeat against the dark.

It had been eleven days since the reactor failure. Eleven days of rationed air, of whispered fears, of crew members who could no longer meet his eyes.

He knew what had to be done. He had always known.

"We vent the aft section," he said quietly, turning to face his first officer. "It's the only way."

Lieutenant Mara Chen's jaw tightened. "Sir, the archives are in the aft section. Everything we've learned on this mission—"

"I know." His voice was steady, but something unreadable flickered in his eyes. "Knowledge is precious. But it isn't worth more than the lives of the people who gathered it."

The silence stretched between them, heavy with everything left unsaid.

Then she nodded.

Hours later, as the Meridian turned toward home, Thorne watched the archive modules drift into the starlight, glittering like scattered memories. Somewhere deep in his chest, something shifted.

They had lost so much. But they had not lost themselves.
