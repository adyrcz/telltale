My uncle Pete sold aluminum siding for thirty-one years and never once, to my knowledge, owned a house that had any.

"Vinyl," he'd say, like it was a dirty word, and then he'd tell you about the Hendersons in Kenosha, who bought the deluxe package in 1979 and were still, he swore, sending him Christmas cards. Nobody ever saw these cards.

The summer I was fourteen, my mother made me ride along with him because she was tired of me and he was tired of being alone, though neither of them said that. We drove a brown Buick LeSabre with a cracked dash and a tape deck that only played the left channel, so for three months I heard half of every Kenny Rogers song.

I was bored most of the time. Also embarrassed, mainly about the hat.

In Racine a woman let us in and gave us lemonade and then cried for twenty minutes about her son, who was in the Navy, and Pete just sat there with his sample case on his knees and let her. He didn't try to sell her anything. In the car afterward I asked why not and he said, "Read the room, kiddo," and turned up the left channel.

She called him two weeks later and bought the whole house. I still don't know if that was the point.
