# Worked example 2: whole story, structure focus

The structural layer is where this skill differs from line-level cleanup tools, and it needs a full story to show. This example works from the **story skeleton** of a hypothetical 4,800-word short story (the skeleton is what the skill builds in Step 1). It shows the five priority checks producing concrete findings, the scorecard, and a revised beat sheet.

**User request:** "Critique the narrative structure of my story. I drafted it with Claude and revised the prose by hand, but beta readers still say it feels 'AI.' Score it."

Because the user named the drafting model, the assessment also checks that model's fingerprint list. It does not infer the model from the text.

---

## Story skeleton (built in Step 1)

**Title:** "The Harbor Ledger" · ~4,800 words · close third person, past tense · literary fiction

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Cast | **Ines Calder**, 34, archivist at a coastal town's historical society. Introduced by a description paragraph (appearance, cardigan, careful hands). Motivation: do her job well → expose the truth. Emotion: contentment → unease → resolve → peace. **Gwen**, her director, cautious. **Mr. Abernathy**, retired history teacher. **Charles Penhallow**, descendant of the town's founder. |
| Relationships | Ines–Gwen: friendly boss, brief tension, reconciled. Ines–Abernathy: mentor. Ines–Penhallow: adversarial → respectful. No community pressure or gossip. |
| Event list (stakes, type) | 1 Ines catalogs donated boxes (1, routine) · 2 finds a 1911 ledger showing the founder acquired harbor land with forged deeds, displacing fishing families (2, discovery) · 3 Abernathy explains the historical context at length (2, conversation) · 4 Gwen warns the society depends on Penhallow Foundation money (3, conversation) · 5 Ines walks the seawall at sunset, thinking about truth and comfort (2, reflection) · 6 decides to present at the centennial council meeting (3, decision) · 7 at the meeting Penhallow objects; Ines speaks about truth; the room goes quiet; Penhallow concedes (3, conversation) · 8 council votes for a memorial plaque; Penhallow thanks her (2, conversation) · 9 epilogue, one year later: plaque unveiled at sunrise (1, ritual) |
| Causal chain | Strictly continuous: each beat follows from the one before. Nothing from outside the chain intervenes. |
| Subplots | None. |
| Central conflict | Ines vs. the town's comfortable founding myth. Resolvable by her alone. |
| Resolution | Ines's speech persuades Penhallow and the council (protagonist choice). External outcome: the plaque. Internal: she "learned the past must be faced." Nothing is left unresolved. |
| Themes | Truth vs. comfort (**stated**: Abernathy's speech in beat 3, Ines's speech in beat 7, the epilogue's closing line "The past, she had learned, was not something to be buried, but something to be faced."). Courage (**stated**, beat 5 interior). |
| Moral frame | Ines's choices: all endorsed. Gwen's caution: framed as fear, then she apologizes. Penhallow: obstacle, then convert. |
| Time map | Linear across about three weeks, plus a one-year epilogue. No flashbacks; the 1911 events are delivered entirely through Abernathy's exposition. |
| Reveal schedule | One reveal (the forgery) at beat 2, delivered when found. Nothing recasts an earlier scene. |
| Perspective | Ines only; deep interior access throughout; dialogue around 20% of text, mostly long speeches. |
| Setting | Three locations (archive, seawall, council chamber). Opening paragraph: fog, gulls, smell of salt and old paper. Sunset at the doubt scene, sunrise at the epilogue. |
| Intertext | None named. One allusion to "an old sea shanty." |

## Assessment

## TellTale assessment: "The Harbor Ledger"
Mode: score (with priority-check detail) · Scope: story, ~4,800 words · Genre/conventions noted: literary fiction; small-town history
Reader-notice rating: **Very high** (confidence: **High**)
Structural layer 5.0/5 (4.0 from the top three rows, +1 for convergence) · Line-level layer: not scored (structure focus requested) · Core features on AI-typical side: 21 of 26

> This is a writing-quality signal about how readers may perceive the prose. It is not evidence of how the text was written.

**Blocking issues:** none found.

**Why:** The beta readers are responding to structure, which hand-revising the prose does not change. The story runs on one causal track at a constant medium pressure, resolves through a speech that converts everyone, and says its theme three times, the last as the final sentence. Each choice is common in human fiction; together, with nothing pulling against them, they place the story in the AI-typical cluster.

### Priority checks

#### S1 Event escalation and pacing: 4/5
- **Stakes curve (telling order):** 1, 2, 2, 3, 2, 3, 3, 2, 1. The peak after the midpoint (3) never exceeds the peak before it (beat 4, also 3).
- **Climax type:** a conversation (the speech) that costs Ines nothing.
- **Event types:** conversation 4 of 9, reflection 1, discovery 1, decision 1, routine 1, ritual 1. No loss, reversal, or physical consequence.
- **Cushioned danger:** Gwen's funding warning (beat 4) is raised and never comes due.
- **Fingerprint note:** you drafted with Claude. Weak event escalation and low event-type diversity are that model's two strongest fingerprints in StoryScope, and the one-year epilogue matches a third (endings that jump forward).

#### S2 Theme handling: 4/5
- Theme stated three times (beats 3, 7, 9). The final sentence is a not-X-but-Y moral, which also counts as a line-level tell.
- Abernathy's beat 3 and Ines's beat 7 are philosophical speeches (AI 59% vs. human 34% for dialogue as debate).
- **Deletion test:** the epilogue line and most of the beat 7 speech fail it; the plaque already makes the point.

#### S3 Agency and resolution: 4/5
- Ines's speech alone converts Penhallow and the council. No outside force, no other character's agenda, no cost.
- Internal resolution stated outright ("she had learned").
- Matches both AI-leaning resolution features: protagonist-choice resolution (69% vs. 46%) and internal-understanding resolution (47% vs. 27%).

#### S4 Moral ambiguity: 4/5
- Every Ines choice is endorsed. Gwen's caution is framed as fear and retracted. Penhallow has no position of his own, only resistance.
- High moral weighting with a clean answer: the story asks a hard question about inherited wrongs and answers it with a plaque.

#### S5 Temporal complexity and revelation: 3/5
- Linear, plus a tidy epilogue. The 1911 history arrives as exposition rather than as scene or delayed disclosure.
- No reveal recasts an earlier scene.

Layer: the top three rows average 4.0. Convergence is 21 of 26 assessable features (81%, above the 70% line, full story), so the layer rises by 1 to 5.0. Overall: Very high (structural layer at 4.0 or above; line layer not scored).

### Fix first
1. **Make the climax cost something and let it partly fail** (S1, S3, S4). Highest leverage: one change lowers three rows.
2. **Cut the epilogue's moral and the speech's thesis** (S2). Quickest win.
3. **Give Penhallow or Gwen a real position** (S4).
4. **Add one subplot with its own stakes** (S6, S10).
5. **Move one disclosure late so it recasts an earlier scene** (S5).

### Structural options (Requires author decision)

#### Flat escalation and costless climax (S1, S3)
- **Option A:** The Foundation pulls funding after Ines's first move; Gwen lays off Ines's part-time assistant. The council meeting then goes against her. The plaque happens later because Penhallow's daughter leaks the ledger to a regional paper. Effect: a reversal, a real cost, and an outside agent closing the conflict. Cost: Ines no longer "wins" by her own speech.
- **Option B:** Keep the speech but let it fail in the room. End the story the night of the meeting, outcome unknown. Effect: removes the converted-antagonist beat and the internal-resolution ending. Cost: no plaque on the page.
- **Option C (lightest touch):** Keep the plot; add a cost (Ines loses her job) and cut the epilogue.

#### Moral certainty (S4)
- **Option A:** The ledger also shows that Ines's own great-grandfather witnessed the forged deeds and was paid for it. She hides that page for most of the story and never fully confesses. Effect: an understandable, wrong, unredeemed choice. Cost: darkens Ines.
- **Option B:** Give Penhallow an argument that lands: the Foundation funds the harbor school the displaced families' descendants now attend. Effect: the opposition has a defensible reason. Cost: the ending can no longer be clean.

#### No subplot; thin social world (S6, S10)
- **Option A:** Ines's brother's commercial fishing permit is up for renewal before the same council. A present-day echo of the 1911 dispossession that is never named as such. Rumor about Ines's research reaches the docks before she speaks. Effect: thematically parallel subplot, community pressure, higher stakes. Cost: about 800 words.

#### Linear, exposition-delivered history (S5)
- **Option A:** Open at the council meeting with Penhallow on his feet, then backfill. Withhold until late that Abernathy is descended from one of the displaced families, which recasts his calm beat 3 explanation. Effect: nonlinear framing for delayed disclosure, plus recontextualization. Cost: restructuring the opening.

## Revised beat sheet (author chose: escalation A, moral A, subplot A, temporal A)

| # | Beat | Stakes | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Council meeting, present: Penhallow rises to object (open in scene) | 3 | confrontation |
| 2 | Three weeks earlier: Ines finds the ledger, and her great-grandfather's signature as witness | 3 | discovery |
| 3 | She removes that page and takes it home | 3 | decision (morally compromised) |
| 4 | Abernathy reads the rest without comment; gives her a box of his mother's papers | 2 | conversation (understated) |
| 5 | Rumor reaches the docks; her brother's permit hearing is moved to the same night | 3 | social pressure |
| 6 | Foundation pulls funding; Gwen lays off Ines's assistant | 4 | loss |
| 7 | Back at the meeting: her speech goes badly; Penhallow's argument about the school lands. Mid-speech she cites a deed from Abernathy's box and realizes his family was among the displaced | 4 | reversal; recontextualizing reveal |
| 8 | Her brother's permit is denied in the same session | 5 | loss |
| 9 | That night, Penhallow's daughter calls: she has photographed the ledger. Ines does not mention the missing page | 4 | outside agency; unresolved |

**New curve:** 3, 3, 3, 2, 3, 4, 4, 5, 4. Peaks after the midpoint now exceed every earlier beat. Event types: 7 distinct. Resolution: external and partial, driven by another character. Theme: never stated. Ines ends having done right and wrong at once. The Abernathy reveal (from the box of papers in beat 4) is placed in beat 7 so it recasts beat 4.

**Overcorrection check:** the revision now has a cold open, a hidden page, and a late reveal. That's enough. Do not also add a dream sequence or a frame narrator to "diversify."
